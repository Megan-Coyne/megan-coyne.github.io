About Me

I’m entering my senior year of college, and as I wrap up my coursework and begin preparing to apply to PhD programs, feel free to use this site to learn a bit more about me! As useful as LinkedIn can be, I often find it lacks personality and originality. As a computer science student, I thought it would be a great opportunity to bring those details to life through a personal portfolio website.

I’m passionate about mathematics in addition to computer science, which is why I’m especially drawn to topics at the intersection of the two. In particular, computational topology and high performance computing have piqued my interest. You can read more about what intrigues me in the interests section.

My most elaborate research experience to date is my honors thesis, which uses Extreme Value Theory to predict municipal financial risk based on corporate bankruptcy. Focusing on Detroit’s 2013 bankruptcy, I model financial extremes in Ford, GM, and Stellantis using the Generalized Extreme Value distribution, analyzing their correlation with city level distress indicators. Through time series and causality analyses, I aim to develop a generalized EVT based model to help anticipate fiscal crises in other industry dependent cities. This project has solidified my passion for research. It has been both challenging and deeply energizing, and I am incredibly grateful for the experience.

In addition to my thesis, I have worked on several impactful projects at the University of Dayton Research Institute. While many remain classified due to our collaboration with the United States Air Force, these experiences have sharpened my skills in software development, data analysis, and working within secure and regulated environments. Among my unclassified work, I have built a tool to streamline research pipelines, developed a GUI and data collection scripts for a change blindness study, and created flexible Docker setups to containerize diverse research projects.

Lastly, an experience that has been profoundly impactful to my growth in both communication and coding is my time at Ford Motor Company. Across two internships, I developed backends for two separate applications, one using Java Spring Boot and the other with Golang. I gained valuable experience in database management and the full software development lifecycle. Working at a large company like Ford taught me how critical clean and efficient code is in collaborative and large scale environments, and helped me become a more thoughtful and effective developer.

Outside of academics, I am the current president of Math Club, Vice President of Member Integrity for my sorority Sigma Kappa, and a consultant with our on-campus consulting group, Flyer Consulting. Beyond school, I enjoy running (I’m training for my first half marathon in September, wish me luck!), discovering new TV shows, and bowling!
